## Key Points

- In classic Hollywood, "The End" was mandatory - massive white letters on black or elegant italics over the final frame
- European cinema preferred "Fin" or "Fine" emphasizing director's signature style
- Directors began using "The End?" to hint at sequels or open endings (common in horror)
- "Diegetic" inscriptions became popular - scratched on walls or drawn in sand
- Modern cinema replaced the phrase with fade-to-black, long credits, or post-credit scenes
- Today, using "The End" is a deliberate technique for retro-nostalgia or finality
