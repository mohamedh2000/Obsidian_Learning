## Key Points
- The core claim is that volume and consistency outperform sophisticated planning in most domains
- "Brute force" here means sustained action without paralysis-by-analysis — not thoughtless repetition
- The fear factor ("genuinely terrifying") comes from the relentlessness being hard to compete against — you can't out-strategize someone who simply won't stop
- High engagement (5K+ likes) suggests this resonates with the "just ship" / "bias toward action" crowd
- Implicit critique of overthinking and planning theater — the idea that strategy becomes procrastination in disguise

### Why This Resonates
In creative and technical fields, there's a common trap where learning *about* the work substitutes for doing the work. This tweet argues that the people who ignore that trap and just accumulate reps tend to win by sheer volume — they iterate faster, fail faster, and compound learning through action. Not a nuanced take, but a useful counterweight to perfectionism.
