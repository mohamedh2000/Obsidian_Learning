# Claude Fable 5 & Agentic Coding: Finding the Unknowns

> On large agentic-coding tasks, plans alone aren't enough — the real skill is cheaply discovering and reducing the "unknowns" before, during, and after implementation.

## Key Points

- Prompts, skills, and context are the "map" you give Claude; unknowns are the real-world constraints of the codebase where work actually happens
- The Rumsfeld Matrix: Known Knowns, Known Unknowns, Unknown Knowns, and Unknown Unknowns
- Pre-implementation patterns to surface unknowns: Blind Spot Pass, Brainstorm & Prototype (HTML), Interview (one question at a time), Reference (point at real source), and front-loading changeable parts of the implementation plan
- During implementation: keep a temporary `implementation-notes.md` for off-plan decisions and edge cases
- Post-implementation: pitch/explainer artifacts for buy-in, and have Claude quiz you to confirm understanding before merge
- Stronger models make cheaply discovering "what you don't know" the most valuable step
