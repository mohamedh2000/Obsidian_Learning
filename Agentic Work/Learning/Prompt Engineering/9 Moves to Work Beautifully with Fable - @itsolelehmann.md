# 9 Moves to Work Beautifully with Fable

> When Fable gets something wrong it's almost always because the prompt was missing information — these 9 moves pull that information out of your head *upfront* so the work comes back right the first time.

## Key Points

1. **Blindspot pass** — before starting something new, ask "what are my unknown unknowns here? teach me enough to prompt you better."
2. **Ask for options** — "give me 10 ways to solve this, cheapest to most ambitious" before deciding what to build.
3. **Throwaway drafts** — when you'll "know it when you see it," ask for 3–4 rough versions; reacting beats describing from scratch.
4. **Let it interview you** — "interview me one question at a time, starting with the ones that would change the whole plan."
5. **Show, don't explain** — point at an existing doc/design/code and say "do it like this."
6. **Plan with big decisions on top** — "put the choices I might change first, boring routine stuff last."
7. **Decision notes file** — "keep a notes file; any time my instructions didn't cover something, write down what you decided and keep going" — you review after.
8. **Quiz yourself** — "summarize what changed, then quiz me; I don't approve until I pass."
9. **Teach me to judge** — if you can't tell good output from bad, have it teach you the domain first (Thariq's color-grading example).

## Notes

Every move does the same job: extract the information before the mistake gets made, not after. Credits Thariq's article.
