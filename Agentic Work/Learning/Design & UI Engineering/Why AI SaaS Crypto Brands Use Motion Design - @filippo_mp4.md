## Key Points
- The core insight: software products are screens, and screen recordings are inherently uncinematic — they lack physical presence, depth, and emotional resonance
- Motion design solves a fundamental marketing problem: how do you make invisible value (data processing, APIs, algorithms) visible and compelling?
- "Something you can feel" — motion design adds emotional texture to abstract functionality, creating visceral understanding where documentation fails
- This explains why high-production motion pieces dominate Product Hunt launches, YC demo days, and crypto project reveals

### The Constraint That Drives Creativity
Software companies face a unique challenge: their product is pixels on glass. Unlike physical products (cars, furniture, gadgets), you can't photograph software in beautiful settings or film it in slow motion. Motion design becomes the only medium capable of:
1. **Visualizing data flows** — showing how information moves through a system
2. **Representing scale** — making "10M requests/second" feel meaningful
3. **Conveying speed** — demonstrating responsiveness without showing lag-inducing screen recordings
4. **Building brand personality** — injecting emotion into functional interfaces

### Industry Implication
This is why motion designers are among the highest-paid creative roles in tech. They're not making decorations — they're solving an existential marketing problem that every software company faces.
