## Key Points
- **Return to analog tools**: Despite sophisticated design software (Figma, Sketch), low-fidelity pencil sketching remains highly effective for ideation
- **Speed of iteration**: Pencil and paper allows faster exploration of concepts without the friction of pixel-perfect digital tools
- **Reduced attachment to early ideas**: Paper sketches feel disposable, making designers more willing to discard and iterate vs. polished Figma mockups
- **Focus on concepts over polish**: Analog tools force attention on structure, flow, and hierarchy rather than colors, fonts, and details

### Why Analog Works in Digital Design
- **Removes software constraints**: No layers, no undo history, no component libraries — just raw ideation
- **Faster feedback loops**: Sketch → critique → discard → resketch happens in minutes, not hours
- **Physical engagement**: Hand-to-paper creates different cognitive patterns than mouse-to-screen, often unlocking creative solutions
- **Democratizes collaboration**: Anyone can sketch on paper; not everyone can navigate Figma

### When to Use Paper vs. Digital
| Paper | Digital |
|-------|---------|
| Early concept exploration | Hi-fi prototyping |
| User flow mapping | Component libraries |
| Rapid wireframing | Responsive variants |
| Collaborative brainstorming | Design handoff |
